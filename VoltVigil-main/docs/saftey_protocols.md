# VoltVigil Safety Protocols & Best Practices

**⚠️ DANGER: LETHAL VOLTAGE HAZARD**
The VoltVigil system interfaces directly with live 230V AC mains electricity. Mishandling these connections can result in severe injury, death, or electrical fires. This document outlines the mandatory safety components and operational protocols for building and testing this project.

---

## 1. High-Voltage (AC) Hardware Protection
To protect the end-user and the local electrical grid, the following components are **mandatory** on the high-voltage side.

* **1A or 2A Glass Fuse (Inline):**
  * **Placement:** Must be the very first component on the incoming AC **LIVE** wire.
  * **Purpose:** If a short circuit occurs within the PZEM-004T, the Relay, or the load, this fuse will blow instantly, severing the connection before the wires can overheat and cause a fire.
* **MOV - Metal Oxide Varistor (7D471K):**
  * **Placement:** Wired directly across the incoming AC **LIVE** (after the fuse) and **NEUTRAL** wires.
  * **Purpose:** Acts as a surge protector. It absorbs high-voltage transients (spikes) from the grid, preventing them from destroying the PZEM-004T or arcing across the relay.
* **Insulation & Connections:**
  * **NO Electrical Tape:** Electrical tape degrades and peels off when exposed to the heat generated by AC loads. You must use **Heat Shrink Tubing** over all soldered AC joints.
  * **Wire Gauge:** Use minimum 1.0mm² (18 AWG) insulated copper wire for all AC routing. Standard breadboard jumper wires will melt instantly if used for AC loads.

---

## 2. Low-Voltage (DC) Hardware Protection
To protect the ESP32 and ensure the relay does not draw dangerous amounts of current from the microcontroller logic pins.

* **The 3.3V Jumper Rule:** * The yellow jumper on the HW-777 Expansion Board must remain on **3.3V**. This ensures the logic pins (S-row) do not exceed the ESP32's maximum tolerance, preventing permanent chip degradation.
* **Common Grounding:**
  * The 12V DC power supply, the ESP32, and all sensors (PZEM, ZMPT, Relay) **must share a common ground**. Failure to do so will result in floating voltages, causing erratic relay triggering or "bogus" sensor data.
* **Signal Isolation:**
  * Keep the 230V AC wires physically separated from the 3.3V/5V DC signal wires. Running them parallel and bundled together can induce electromagnetic interference (EMI) that crashes the ESP32 via the I2C (OLED) or ADC (ZMPT101B) lines.

---

## 3. Operational & Testing Rules

### The "One Power Source" Rule
**Never** connect the ESP32 to your computer via USB while the system is plugged into 230V AC mains. 
* Ground loops between your laptop, the USB cable, and the AC mains can destroy your computer's motherboard if a fault occurs in the VoltVigil circuit. 
* **Workflow:** Flash the code via USB $\rightarrow$ Unplug USB $\rightarrow$ Plug in 12V DC Adapter $\rightarrow$ Plug in 230V AC Mains.

### The "One Hand" Rule
When using a multimeter to probe the live circuit (e.g., checking the AC voltage across the relay), keep one hand in your pocket or behind your back. This prevents a potential electrical shock from traveling across your chest and through your heart.

### Enclosure Guidelines
If housing VoltVigil in a 3D-printed or plastic junction box:
* Ensure adequate ventilation. The 12V Relay coil and the PZEM-004T will generate mild heat during continuous operation.
* Use mechanical cable glands to secure the incoming and outgoing AC power cords so they cannot be accidentally yanked out of the internal screw terminals.
